## Observations

### Verification
`analyze.py selftest` checks four properties, and for each a deliberate break must fail.
`sdt.delrmrm` (×10) equals the probe's I1 product sample for sample (the same at 7.35 m instead of 7.291 m: caught).
I1 at 96 kHz matches the original at 96 kHz, where it is anchored (against the original at 48 kHz: +6.29 dB, caught).
Its level before the compressor stays within 0.5 dB between 48 and 96 kHz (the original: +6.00 dB, caught).
Its state stays below a tenth of the note's own integral after ten minutes of floor and DC (the original: 187 against 65 at 96 kHz, caught).

### The original drifts, and the drift changes the effect
With the DC measured on track 1 (3.22e-6, about −110 dBFS) the original integrator's state grows linearly: 10 after one minute, 48 after five, 95 after ten at 48 kHz, twice as fast at 96 kHz.
The integral of the note itself is about 33 at 48 kHz and 65 at 96 kHz: after a few minutes of an idle input the drift is larger than the music.
When the note arrives after five minutes (96 kHz), the triple product is 7.3 dB louder and correlates at 0.88 with the plain self-ring-modulation 10·x[n−D]·x: the integral has become a constant, and delRM's channels 2 and 4 have turned into another effect.
The compressor hides the level (+1.6 dB after it) but not the change of character.
So in Pd the sound of these channels depended on how long the patch had been running.

### A DC blocker in front of an integrator is a leaky integrator
I2 (`fi.dcblockerat(5)` then `fi.integrator`) does not drift: its state reads 0.022, 0.036, 0.025, 0.026, 0.028 at 1, 10, 60, 300, 600 s, the same values as I1 with fc = 5 Hz.
The blocker's zero at z = 1 cancels the integrator's pole at z = 1, and what remains is the blocker's own pole: a leaky integrator with a 5 Hz corner.
What I2 does not fix is the rate: its level still rises by 6 dB from 48 to 96 kHz, because the sum of samples is not normalised to time.

### The leaky integrator, normalised
I1 is `sfi.leakyint(1) : *(96000)`: the integral in seconds, which forgets below 1 Hz, scaled to the original's sum of samples at 96 kHz, the rate SSCDO#2 is played at.
It is flat across rates (−0.01 dB from 48 to 96 kHz), bounded, and on a fresh start within 0.3 dB of the original at 96 kHz.
Its corner sits far below the clarinet's 29.7 Hz fundamental, so above it the integral is the original's.
It is `sdt.delrmint`, and the triple product is `sdt.delrmrm`.

### Why 96000 and not `ma.SR`
Scaling by `ma.SR` instead of 96000 cancels the `/SR` inside `sfi.leakyint` and gives back a sum of samples: Davide's mechanism, made stable, identical at 96 kHz.
At another rate it differs, measured on the note with the original sum of samples:

| | 48 kHz | 96 kHz | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| before the compressor | +14.32 dBFS | +20.32 dBFS | +6.00 dB |
| after the compressor | −19.31 dBFS | −19.52 dBFS | −0.21 dB |

The 11:1 compressor absorbs the level but not the depth of its work: with 6 dB less at its input it reduces less, and the envelope of the triple product is shaped differently.
The question is what the specification is: the mechanism (a sum of samples) or the sound as Davide heard it at 96 kHz.
Chosen with Giuseppe: the sound, anchored at 96 kHz, so that delRM behaves the same in a 48 kHz rehearsal, in *Studio sul Corpo d'Ombra #4*, or in a student's session — the rule already applied to Di Scipio's constants at 44.1 kHz.
