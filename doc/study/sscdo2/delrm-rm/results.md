## Observations

### Verification
`analyze.py selftest` checks two properties, and the original integrator is the mutation that must fail them: the level before the compressor stays within 0.5 dB between 48 and 96 kHz (I1: pass; I0: +6.00 dB, caught), and the integrator's state stays below a tenth of the note's own integral after ten minutes of floor and DC (I1: pass; I0: 95.3 against 32.6, caught).

### The original drifts, and the drift changes the effect
With the DC measured on track 1 (3.22e-6, about −110 dBFS) the original integrator's state grows linearly: 10 after one minute, 48 after five, 95 after ten at 48 kHz, twice as fast at 96 kHz.
The integral of the note itself is about 33: after a few minutes of an idle input the drift is larger than the music.
When the note arrives after five minutes, the triple product is 7 dB louder and correlates at 0.88 with the plain self-ring-modulation 10·x[n−D]·x: the integral has become a constant, and delRM's channels 2 and 4 have turned into another effect.
The compressor hides the level (+1.6 dB after it) but not the change of character.
So in Pd the sound of these channels depended on how long the patch had been running.

### A DC blocker in front of an integrator is a leaky integrator
I2 (`fi.dcblockerat(5)` then `fi.integrator`) does not drift: its state reads 0.022, 0.036, 0.025, 0.026, 0.028 at 1, 10, 60, 300, 600 s, the same values as I1 with fc = 5 Hz.
The blocker's zero at z = 1 cancels the integrator's pole at z = 1, and what remains is the blocker's own pole: a leaky integrator with a 5 Hz corner.
What I2 does not fix is the rate: its level still rises by 6 dB from 48 to 96 kHz, because the sum of samples is not normalised to time.

### The leaky integrator, normalised
I1 is flat across rates (−0.01 dB from 48 to 96 kHz), bounded (its state never exceeds 0.08 in ten minutes), and on a fresh start within 0.3 dB of the original at 48 kHz, where its normalisation anchors it.
Its corner (1 Hz here) sits well below the clarinet's 29.7 Hz fundamental, so above it the integral is the original's.
The 48 kHz anchor is provisional: if the performance ran at another rate, the anchor follows it.
