## Observations

These notes are written by hand from the tables above; `analyze.py measure` keeps them when it regenerates the tables.

### Verification

`analyze.py selftest` passes, and each deliberate mutation fails as it should: A mistuned by 1 Hz fails on peak frequency, A with the lowpass 1 Hz above the highpass fails on peak, bandwidth and the +½ octave point, and C with its upper edge moved by 1 Hz fails on the edge.
A and B match the analytic |H|² = 1/(2 + r⁴⁸ + r⁻⁴⁸) to 2·10⁻⁴ dB wherever the theory lies above −150 dB, which confirms the prompt's analysis: peak −6.02 dB, half-power band F/1.0374 … F·1.0374 (7.35 % of F, Q ≈ 13.6), −72.2 dB at ±½ octave, −144.5 dB at ±1 octave.
The −3 dB edges are the half-power points (−3.0103 dB), the definition shared by the analytic formula and by `fi.bandpass`; at −3.000 dB the band is 0.19 % narrower, which the first self-test run exposed.
The generated code shows the two library trees in use: A carries `basics.lib` 1.15.0 and 25 recursive states (24 direct-form biquads plus si.smoo), B carries 1.23.4 and 49 states (24 SVF sections of two states each, plus si.smoo).

### Steady state: A and B are the same filter

In double precision A and B agree to 1.8·10⁻¹² on the impulse response and are indistinguishable in every M1 and M2 column.
The steady renders of A and B differ at about −160 dB re their level, so B is a transparent replacement for A in Davide's use (double precision, a slider held still).

### The measurement floor

Both A and B show a floor near −185 dB beyond ±2 octaves, where the theory falls to −289 dB.
That floor belongs to the double-precision recursion of the impulse response, and it is the same for both structures.
C reaches the same floor already at ±¼ octave, where its theory is −330 dB: every C cell below about −180 dB reads the floor, and the filter itself goes lower.

### C has the same band edges and a very different shape

With the edges pinned to A's half-power points, C has a flat top across the band and skirts that fall to the floor within a quarter octave, where A is only 36 dB down.
C passes 0.55 dB less noise power than A at the same peak (Σh² −41.27 against −40.72 dB), because A's gentler skirts add more energy than C's flat top.
The cost of the brick-wall shape is time: C's group delay at F is 680 ms against A's 117 ms, and its impulse response takes 4.95 s to fall 60 dB against A's 0.31 s.
The physics is in the poles.
A gets its narrow band from two 24th-order skirts overlapping at the same fc, so each pole has a Q of at most 1/(2·sin(π/48)) ≈ 7.6.
C gets it from a lowpass prototype of width 7.16 Hz mapped to 97 Hz, so its slowest pole pair has a Q of about 208 (the same sin(π/48) damping, applied to a 7.16 Hz width instead of a 97 Hz cutoff).
On noise, C should therefore sound more like a sustained tone with a slow, smeared attack; this is a prediction from the numbers, and the renders exist to test it by ear.

### The glissando (cue 2)

On the real glissando, 15 Hz in 120 s through si.smoo, A and B differ by −74.7 dB RMS and −73.2 dB in the worst 1 s window, with no transient: the two structures behave alike under slow modulation.
C tracks the same glissando with a lag of about its group delay (0.68 s ≈ 0.085 Hz at 0.125 Hz/s, small against a 7 Hz band) and a level 0.5 dB below A, which matches its lower noise gain.
The level change expected over the glissando for a constant relative bandwidth is +0.63 dB (the M1 noise gains at 97.44 and 112.665 Hz differ by exactly that); level taken over a few seconds of 7 Hz-wide noise varies by about ±1 dB, so the tables give no short-window level trend.

### The step (secondary)

A step of 15 Hz (two bandwidths) smoothed only by si.smoo (τ = 22.7 ms) is where A and B part.
The difference signal reaches −17.5 dB re A in the first 100 ms after the step and −27.6 dB in the next, then falls below −100 dB within 0.4 s.
With a tone at the old centre, A overshoots by +1.3 dB in the first 100 ms (−7.82 against −9.10 dBFS steady) while B does not (−9.27 dBFS); B's transition is the monotonic one of the two.
On noise the 20 ms crest after the step (+6.40 dB) sits close to the crest of the unstepped noise (+5.37 dB), so neither A nor B produces a transient that stands out of the noise itself.
C behaves as its group delay predicts: a tone at the old centre keeps sounding at full level for about 0.5 s after the step, and a tone at the new centre needs about 0.6 s to arrive.

### The #262 warning

A unit 1 kHz tone, more than three octaves above the band, leaks through the step at −124 dBFS peak in A and −123 dBFS in B, from a steady −285 / −295 dBFS.
So the leakage the commit message describes is real, and the direct form shows it too, at the same size.
At these levels it has no audible consequence in double precision; on the glissando neither A nor B leaks above −222 dBFS.
C, whose SVF bandpass sections see the tone through the whole cascade, reads −136 dBFS peak on the glissando and −129 dBFS after the step.

### Level

Davide's true output (A doubled by the two identical oscillators, then `fi.dcblockerat(20)`, LMO Volume 1) is −39.21 dBFS RMS with a −26.4 dBFS peak on the 20 s steady render.
The prediction from the noise RMS (−4.77 dBFS), A's noise gain (−40.72 dB) and the doubling (+6.02 dB) is −39.47 dBFS; the 0.26 dB gap is within the statistical spread of 20 s of 7 Hz-wide noise (about ±0.4 dB), and not separately explained.
The listening renders are matched to −20 dBFS RMS, so the matched A and B files carry +25.06 dB of gain over the bare filter and C +25.40 dB.

### Open points and uncertainty

- Everything here is double precision at 48 kHz, as scoped; float behaviour, other sample rates and CPU cost were not measured.
- The glissando uses a sample-accurate ramp; in Pd the slider reaches the external as control messages, block by block, before si.smoo, so the real control signal is a staircase smoothed by si.smoo. The glissando's per-block steps are then about 0.0002 Hz, far smaller than the step that separated A and B here, but that was not measured.
- The step is a synthetic stress case; the cue uses the glissando.
