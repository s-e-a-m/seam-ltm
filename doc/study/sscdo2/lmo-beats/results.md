## Observations

### Verification
`analyze.py selftest` checks three properties and breaks each on purpose: the level at d = 0 equals one oscillator's (two separate `no.multinoise` calls fail it at +3.01 dB, because the fixed seed makes them identical); the two bands of channel 0 are independent (a band against itself fails it at r = 1); the beat bump sits at d (the same spectrum read against d = 30 fails it).
The first version of the third check looked for the maximum of the envelope spectrum and failed on the real signal, which is how the shape of a noise beat came to light (below).

### What a beat between two noise bands looks like
A single band of noise already fluctuates: at d = 0 its envelope spectrum is a low-pass hump that falls 10 dB by 11.5 Hz.
Two independent bands d apart add a broad bump centred on d, about twice a band's width across, riding on that hump and weaker than its peak.
At d = 20 the envelope spectrum is −6.5 dB at 20 Hz, against −22.3 dB for d = 0: 16 dB of beat, spread from about 15 to 27 Hz.
The absolute maximum of the spectrum stays near 4 Hz at every d; the beat shows only against the baseline.
Its centroid reads 21.9 Hz for d = 20 and 40.4 Hz for d = 40; below d = 10 the excess is too small for the centroid to mean anything (8.7 Hz for d = 3).

### Where the beat emerges
| d | excess at d over d = 0 | reading |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Hz | +0.1 dB | inside the band's own fluctuation: no beat |
| 7 Hz | +0.9 dB | barely |
| 10 Hz | +3.0 dB | emerging |
| 20 Hz | +15.8 dB | clear |
| 40 Hz | +34.5 dB | clear; at f = 97.44 the bands sit at 77 and 117 Hz, almost a fifth apart |

The estimate made before measuring (a beat above about 7 Hz, the band's width) was optimistic: the envelope of the band carries energy up to about 11 Hz, and the beat stands out from about 10–15 Hz.
Below that, a second band makes the sound wider and more restless, not beating.

### Level and channels
The RMS stays within 0.2 dB of −48.3 dBFS at every d: the 1/√2 compensation holds, because the two bands are independent.
The study runs at 48 kHz: since 2026-10-01 `sdt.lmo` carries the 96 kHz density anchor (`sdt.lmodens`, √(48000/96000)), so every absolute level here is 3.01 dB below the first run (−45.3 dBFS), and every relative number is unchanged.
The renders in `renders/` are those of the first run, kept as heard on 2026-09-29; they are normalised to −20 dBFS RMS, so the anchor changes nothing in them but the gain recorded in `render-log.md`.
Adjacent channels stay uncorrelated (|r| < 0.07) at every d.
